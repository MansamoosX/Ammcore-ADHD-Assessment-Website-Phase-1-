ADHD Diagnostic Assessment
Structured Interview Guide

This guide provides a standardised framework for conducting ADHD diagnostic assessments at Ammcore Clinical Assessments. Follow each section in order. Use the probe questions to explore each domain thoroughly. Do not skip sections — every patient receives the same standard of assessment regardless of presentation.

Opening — Consent & Identity

~5 mins
Step 1 — Identity Verification
Ask the patient to show photo ID to the camera before the session begins.
Say to patient
"Before we begin, I just need to confirm your identity — could you hold your photo ID up to the camera for me? A passport or driving licence is fine."
Record the type of ID shown in the pre-assessment checklist and diagnostic report.
Step 2 — Capacity & Consent
Confirm the patient understands the nature and purpose of the assessment and consents to proceed.
Say to patient
"Today I'll be conducting a diagnostic assessment for ADHD. This will involve me asking you a range of questions about your experiences, your history, and how things affect your day-to-day life. Everything you share is confidential. The session will last around 90 minutes. Do you have any questions before we start, and are you happy to proceed?"
If the patient has not signed the clinical disclaimer, obtain verbal consent now and record it in the diagnostic report under Capacity & Consent.
Step 3 — Set the Tone
Reassure the patient before moving into clinical questioning.
Say to patient
"There are no right or wrong answers — I'm simply here to understand your experiences as clearly as possible. Please take your time, and feel free to tell me if you'd like me to rephrase anything or if you need a moment."
2

Presenting Complaint

~10 mins
Opening Question
"What has brought you to seek an assessment today?"
  • How long have you been aware of these difficulties?
  • Was there a specific moment or event that prompted you to seek help?
  • Has anyone else — a partner, friend, employer — commented on these difficulties?
  • How are these difficulties affecting your daily life right now?
Let the patient speak freely for the first 2–3 minutes. Do not interrupt. Note the themes they raise unprompted — these are the most diagnostically significant.
Main Concerns
"What would you say are your three biggest day-to-day challenges?"
  • At work or in education
  • In relationships or socially
  • With managing yourself — organisation, time, tasks

DSM-5 Criteria Reference — Keep Visible Throughout

DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria for ADHD

Requires ≥6 symptoms (≥5 for adults 17+) in either or both domains, present for ≥6 months, onset before age 12, present in ≥2 settings, causing functional impairment.

Inattention Symptoms

  • Fails to give close attention to detail / makes careless mistakes
  • Difficulty sustaining attention in tasks or play
  • Does not seem to listen when spoken to directly
  • Does not follow through on instructions / fails to finish tasks
  • Difficulty organising tasks and activities
  • Avoids tasks requiring sustained mental effort
  • Loses things necessary for tasks
  • Easily distracted by extraneous stimuli
  • Forgetful in daily activities

Hyperactivity / Impulsivity Symptoms

  • Fidgets with hands/feet or squirms in seat
  • Leaves seat when expected to remain seated
  • Runs or climbs in inappropriate situations (in adults: feeling restless)
  • Unable to play or engage in leisure activities quietly
  • Often "on the go" or acts as if "driven by a motor"
  • Talks excessively
  • Blurts out answers before question is completed
  • Difficulty waiting their turn
  • Interrupts or intrudes on others
3

Inattention Domain — DIVA 5.0

~25 mins
Framing — Say This First
Say to patient
"I'm going to ask you about a range of specific experiences. For each one, I'd like to know whether it applies to you now as an adult, and also whether it was something you experienced as a child — say, before the age of 12."
Inattention — Item 1
"Do you often fail to give close attention to detail, or make careless mistakes in your work, studies, or other activities?"
  • Can you give me a recent example?
  • Does this happen even when the stakes are high?
  • Do others point out your errors more than you notice them yourself?
  • Was this the case at school as a child?
Inattention — Item 2
"Do you have difficulty keeping your attention on tasks or activities for a sustained period — such as reading, meetings, or conversations?"
  • How long can you typically focus before your mind wanders?
  • Does this change if you find something interesting — can you hyperfocus?
  • What strategies have you developed to cope with this?
  • Did teachers comment on your concentration as a child?
Inattention — Item 3
"Do people often say you don't seem to be listening when they speak to you directly?"
  • Do you find your mind is elsewhere even when you want to listen?
  • Do you miss parts of conversations and have to ask people to repeat themselves?
  • Has this caused problems in relationships or at work?
Inattention — Item 4
"Do you often start tasks but struggle to follow through and finish them?"
  • Is this across all types of tasks, or specific ones?
  • Do you tend to move on to new things before completing what you started?
  • How does this affect you at work or at home?
Inattention — Item 5
"Do you have difficulty organising tasks, activities, or your time?"
  • Do you struggle to prioritise what needs to be done first?
  • Do you miss deadlines or appointments?
  • Is your living or working environment organised or chaotic?
  • Do you rely heavily on lists, alarms, or other people to stay on track?
Inattention — Item 6
"Do you avoid or put off tasks that require a lot of mental effort, like paperwork, reports, or admin?"
  • How long do you tend to put things off?
  • Does this happen even when the consequences are serious?
  • What happens when a deadline is approaching — do you manage to complete it?
Note the difference between avoidance due to difficulty and avoidance due to disinterest — both are relevant but may indicate different profiles.
Inattention — Item 7
"Do you frequently lose or misplace things you need — keys, phone, wallet, documents?"
  • How often does this happen?
  • Has it ever caused significant problems?
  • Have you developed any systems to prevent it?
Inattention — Item 8
"Are you easily distracted by things around you — sounds, movement, other people?"
  • What types of distraction affect you most?
  • Do your own thoughts distract you as much as external things?
  • Do you have to work in specific conditions to concentrate?
Inattention — Item 9
"Are you forgetful in daily activities — forgetting appointments, errands, returning calls, paying bills?"
  • How often does this happen in a typical week?
  • Has forgetting things caused problems in relationships or work?
  • Do you forget things even when you've just been told?
4

Hyperactivity & Impulsivity Domain — DIVA 5.0

~20 mins
Hyperactivity — Item 1
"Do you fidget — tapping your hands or feet, bouncing your leg, or finding it hard to sit still?"
  • Is this something others notice about you?
  • Does it get worse in certain situations — meetings, waiting rooms, long journeys?
  • Were you like this as a child?
Hyperactivity — Item 2
"Do you find it difficult to stay in your seat when you're expected to — at work, in meetings, at social occasions?"
  • Do you make excuses to get up and move around?
  • As a child, were you frequently told to sit down or stay in your seat?
Hyperactivity — Item 3
"As an adult, do you feel an inner sense of restlessness — like you can't switch off or slow down?"
  • How would you describe it — physical, mental, or both?
  • Does it affect your ability to relax or wind down in the evenings?
  • Do others describe you as always being "on the go"?
In adults, physical hyperactivity often presents as internal restlessness rather than overt physical movement. Explore this carefully.
Hyperactivity — Item 4
"Do you find it hard to relax or engage in quiet leisure activities?"
  • Can you sit and watch a film, read a book, or just rest without needing to do something else?
  • Do you tend to multitask even when you don't need to?
Hyperactivity — Item 5
"Do people describe you as always being busy, always doing something — like you're driven by a motor?"
  • Do you take on too many things at once?
  • Do you feel uncomfortable doing nothing?
  • What happens when you're forced to wait — in a queue, at an appointment?
Hyperactivity — Item 6
"Do you talk excessively — more than others in most situations?"
  • Do you often speak before thinking?
  • Do others tell you that you dominate conversations?
  • Do you find it hard to stop talking even when you know you should?
Impulsivity — Item 7
"Do you often blurt out answers or comments before someone has finished speaking?"
  • Do you finish other people's sentences?
  • Do you often say things and immediately regret them?
  • Has this caused problems in relationships or at work?
Impulsivity — Item 8
"Do you find it difficult to wait your turn — in conversations, queues, or situations where patience is needed?"
  • How do you feel when you have to wait?
  • Do you become frustrated or agitated quickly?
Impulsivity — Item 9
"Do you act impulsively — making decisions or doing things without thinking them through first?"
  • Can you give me an example — spending, relationships, career decisions?
  • Have impulsive decisions caused significant problems for you?
  • Do you often feel regret after acting impulsively?
Explore impulsivity across multiple domains — financial, sexual, substance use, relationships, and risk-taking behaviours. Each is clinically significant and should be recorded sensitively.
5

Childhood Onset — DSM-5 Criterion B

Part of sections 3 & 4
Key Criterion
Symptoms must have been present before age 12. Explore this for every domain above.
  • Were these difficulties present at primary school? Secondary school?
  • What did teachers say about you — in reports or in person?
  • Did you get into trouble at school? For what?
  • Did family members notice anything different about you as a child?
  • Did you struggle more than other children with sitting still, concentrating, or following instructions?
School reports from the intake form are invaluable here. Reference them if available — "I can see from your school reports that..."
Informant Corroboration
If an informant form was received, reference key points during this section.
If informant available
"I can see from the information provided by [informant name] that [key observation]. Does that match with how you remember things?"
If no informant information was received, note this in the report. It does not invalidate the assessment but should be documented.
6

History & Background

~15 mins
Psychiatric History
"Have you ever been diagnosed with any mental health condition, or received any psychiatric treatment?"
  • Have you ever been hospitalised for mental health reasons?
  • Have you experienced depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, or any other condition?
  • Are you currently receiving any mental health support?
Medical History
"Do you have any current or past medical or surgical conditions?"
  • Are you taking any regular medications?
  • Do you have any known allergies — particularly to medications?
  • Have you had any significant surgeries or hospital admissions?
Substance Use
"Can you tell me about your use of alcohol and any other substances?"
  • How often do you drink alcohol, and how much typically?
  • Do you use cannabis, or any other recreational substances?
  • Have you ever felt that substance use has been a problem for you?
Ask sensitively and without judgement. Substance use is common in undiagnosed ADHD as a coping mechanism and is clinically important to document.
Family History
"Is there any family history of ADHD, autism, or other mental health conditions?"
  • Do any siblings, parents, or close relatives have similar difficulties to you?
  • Have any family members been formally diagnosed?
Developmental & Social History
"Tell me a bit about your background — growing up, school, and work."
  • Were there any complications at birth or early developmental concerns?
  • How did you get on at school — academically and socially?
  • What has your work history looked like?
  • What is your current living situation?
  • Are you in a relationship? How have relationships been for you?
Sleep
"How is your sleep?"
  • Do you have difficulty getting to sleep? Staying asleep?
  • How many hours do you typically get?
  • Is your mind active at night — racing thoughts, images?
Driving
"Do you drive, and if so, do you feel your difficulties affect your ability to drive safely?"
  • Any accidents, near misses, or points on your licence?
DVLA requires disclosure if ADHD affects driving ability. Record the patient's response and include in the report.
7

Mental State Examination & Risk Screen

~10 mins
Mental State Examination
Observe and record throughout the session. Do not leave this to the end.
  • Appearance: Dress, grooming, eye contact
  • Behaviour: Engagement, cooperation, rapport
  • Speech: Rate, volume, pressure, coherence
  • Mood: Patient's report of their mood
  • Affect: Observed emotional tone — congruent, reactive, flat
  • Thought process: Logical, tangential, circumstantial, flight of ideas
  • Thought content: Main themes, preoccupations
  • Perceptual disturbances: Hallucinations, illusions — present/absent
  • Insight: Awareness of difficulties and their impact
  • Cognition: Apparent memory, concentration during the session
Risk to Self
"Sometimes when people are struggling, they can have thoughts of harming themselves or not wanting to be here. Have you experienced anything like that?"
  • Have you ever had thoughts of suicide or self-harm?
  • Are you having any thoughts like that currently?
  • Have you ever acted on those thoughts?
If the patient discloses current suicidal ideation or active self-harm: do not proceed with the assessment. Provide crisis resources immediately (NHS 111 mental health option / 999 / A&E). Contact the clinical lead. Document everything.
Risk to Others
"Have you ever had thoughts of harming anyone else, or has anyone ever been concerned about your behaviour towards others?"
If any risk to others is disclosed: escalate immediately to the clinical lead before concluding the session.
Safeguarding
Consider throughout the session whether any safeguarding concerns arise — for the patient or others, including children.
If a safeguarding concern is identified: escalate immediately. Do not wait until after the session.
8

Closing the Session

~5 mins
Invite Final Thoughts
"Is there anything else you'd like to share that we haven't covered, or anything you feel is important for me to know?"
Patients often disclose the most clinically significant information at this point. Allow enough time for a genuine response.
Explain Next Steps
Say to patient
"Thank you so much for your time today — you've been really open and that helps enormously. Your report will be reviewed by our consultant clinician and delivered to you securely within [7 / 3] working days. If you have any questions in the meantime, please don't hesitate to contact us at info@ammcoreassessments.co.uk. We'll be in touch soon."
After the Session
Complete the following immediately after the call ends:
  • Open the ADHD Diagnostic Report form and complete all sections while the session is fresh
  • Record all rating scale scores (ASRS, DIVA, WURS-25, WFIRS, AQ-10, BDI, BAI)
  • Complete the Post-Assessment section of the Pre-Assessment Checklist
  • Print report as PDF (use Print / Save PDF button in the report form) and email to booking@ammcoreassessments.co.uk for consultant review — do not send to patient directly
Aim to have the report drafted within 24 hours. The Standard deadline is 7 working days and Express is 3 working days from the assessment date.
